Conduct a careful visual inspection of the property.
Discuss any red flags with the seller and recommend further investigation. Real estate licensees are responsible for not only what they know, but also what they should have known.
Disclose any material facts, red flags, and results of investigations to the buyer.
Have the seller complete a seller property disclosure form without your assistance.
Be careful about offering opinions or giving advice. Make only statements of fact that have been verified.
On request, provide a list of experts from which the buyer can make a selection. In addition, refer clients to the excellent Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) internet resources.
As a regular routine, keep written records of all key activities, including discussions with buyers, the dates they occurred, and your responses to buyers' questions.
Follow up oral representations with a written memorandum, delivered by mail or email. Keep copies of all correspondence, including electronic messages. Note: Because they do not provide easily recordable copies and may be so brief as to be easily misconstrued, phone text messages and postings on social media websites (Facebook, Twitter, and so on) are not adequate memoranda of a conversation. Such communications should always be followed up with an email or a letter.
