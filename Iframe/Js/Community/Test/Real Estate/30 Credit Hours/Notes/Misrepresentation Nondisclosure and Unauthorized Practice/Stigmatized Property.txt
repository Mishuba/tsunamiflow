Stigmatized property is property that may be perceived by buyers and lessees as having lesser value or desirability due to events or occurrences that took place on or about the property, such as a murder or suicide.

Failing to disclose a stigma leaves the real estate licensee open to a charge of misrepresentation, unless state law explicitly exempts the obligation to disclose certain stigmas.

In Section 40-57-740, no cause of action may arise against a real estate licensee for failure to disclose that the owner was infected with a contagious disease that is unlikely to be transmitted through occupancy of a dwelling place or that an occupant of the property died on the premise.

Note: HUD has declared it unlawful to disclose that a current or former occupant of a property had AIDS or was HIV positive. In addition, state law may forbid such disclosure.

Similarly, a licensee is not required to discuss or disclose an off-site condition or hazard that does not directly impact the property being transferred, or to disclose any psychological impact that does not materially affect the physical condition of the property being transferred.

Licensees should be aware that they are still responsible for truthfully responding to direct questions from their buyers with regards to psychological impacts, offsite conditions, or stigmas associated with the real estate.

Real estate licensees can avoid misrepresenting stigmatized property by

verifying the truthfulness of the stigma,
checking state law for any exemptions,
exploring the stigma's effect on the price of the property or a buyer's willingness to purchase the property, and
discussing the disclosure with the sellers.
