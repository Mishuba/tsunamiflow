A buyer representation agreement provides for the exclusive right to represent the buyer for a certain period, to assist the buyer in locating real property of a specific nature, and to negotiate terms for its acquisition acceptable to the buyer. The agreement may also cover a possible dual agency, possible conflicts with other buyer-clients, compensation options, an optional retainer fee, and binding arbitration.
  In a transaction in which the licensee represents a buyer, the seller's agent should be informed of the buyer-agent arrangement at first contact, again at the time the appointment is made, and once more when the offer is presented. The closing or escrow agent should also understand the representation arrangements.
  State disclosure requirements, which are likely to include statutory agency disclosure forms, must be met.


Compensation 
Payment of a commission or other form of compensation does not necessarily establish agency. It makes no difference which party makes the payment: the seller, the buyer, or both. Licensee compensation is disbursed at closing as part of the transaction just like any other closing cost. The closing document should clearly show two payments—one marked "on behalf of the seller" to the seller's agent's office and the other marked "on behalf of the buyer" to the buyer's agent's office.

Advantages of Buyer Brokerage 
Buyer agency offers some advantages to both buyer and seller, including
no seller liability for subagents; and 
sole representation for the buyer.
