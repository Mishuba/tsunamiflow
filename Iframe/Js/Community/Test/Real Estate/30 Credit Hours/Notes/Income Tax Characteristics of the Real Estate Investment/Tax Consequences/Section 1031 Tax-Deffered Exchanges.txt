The Internal Revenue Code allows a taxpayer to exchange property held for investment or for use in a trade or business, for property solely of like kind, and to defer the gain realized until disposition of the property acquired. This means that the taxpayer may exchange investment for investment for the transaction to qualify as a 1031 tax-deferred exchange. This does not mean that the taxpayer must exchange a rental house for a rental house, or an industrial property for industrial property. Instead, like kind refers to the use of the property for investment, not its quality or nature. It is perfectly acceptable to exchange a piece of vacant land held for investment for a retail property to be held for investment and defer the gain.

In 1984, the IRS sanctioned a "delayed exchange" based on the old Starker cases. Under current law, a taxpayer may hire a qualified intermediary to accomplish such an exchange. This method allows for what appears to be a sale and later repurchase to qualify for 1031 tax-deferred treatment.

Under the delayed exchange rules, a taxpayer may structure an exchange through a qualified intermediary. The taxpayer must identify a replacement property within 45 days of closing on the disposition, and must actually acquire an identified property not later than 180 days from disposition.

There are several special income tax considerations beyond gain deferral. The taxpayer has a "carry forward" basis, meaning the basis transfers from the previous investment. The taxpayer is also required to continue the depreciation schedule from the previous asset.
