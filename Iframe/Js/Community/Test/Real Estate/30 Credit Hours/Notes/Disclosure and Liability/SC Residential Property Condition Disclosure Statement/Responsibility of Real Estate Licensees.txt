The South Carolina Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement found in the South Carolina Code of Laws Title 27, Chapter 50, Article 20 requires that owners of one- to four-family dwellings provide certain statements about the condition of the property before the two parties sign a purchase agreement.

Applies to these owners: single-family home, duplex, triplex, four-plex (quadraplex)

Does NOT apply to these owners: commercial, industrial, or dwellings that consist of five or more units.

The owner is solely responsible for making the disclosures, not the real estate licensee.

Licensees should remember that the real estate contract, not the owner disclosure, controls what property transfers from owner to purchaser. If conditions change, the owner must promptly correct the disclosure. Two notes about the disclosure statement:

As owner, do you have any actual knowledge of problems, including present defects, malfunctions, damages, conditions, or characteristics?
Note the presence of wood-destroying insects, dry rot, fungus, and what has not been repaired. The form does not inquire about past repairs to damage—only if there is a current problem.
The real estate licensee is responsible for disclosing material facts about the property when and if the licensee is knowledgeable or should be knowledgeable about the issue.

Licensees should encourage owners to make complete and honest disclosures. They should encourage owners to update the disclosure statement if problems arise during the transaction.
