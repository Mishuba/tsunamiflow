Once the owners sign the disclosure, they acknowledge that they are responsible for making appropriate disclosures and that they may be held liable for conditions that they knew about and did not disclose. If the owner is aware of a problem and fails to disclose it, the owner may be liable for making an intentional or negligent misrepresentation. The seller may be liable to the buyer for actual damages, court costs, and attorney fees.

The owner can respond in one of the following three ways on the disclosure statement:

Yes: The owner is aware of a problem.
No: The owner is NOT aware of a problem.
No Representation: The owner essentially is saying, "I am not saying, 'yes, there is a problem,' and I am not saying, 'no, there is no problem.' I am just not saying."
Checking "no representation" provides no relief of liability to sellers. If any question is answered "no representation," the owner is stating that the owner is making no representation of the property, but the owner may still have a duty to disclose information that is known or should have been known. Either the owner knows or the owner does not know. If the owner uses "no representation," buyers are likely to consider if the owner is hiding something.

If the owners know about a problem, they should check "yes." When the owners know about a problem and check "no representation," the owners are liable for nondisclosure.
