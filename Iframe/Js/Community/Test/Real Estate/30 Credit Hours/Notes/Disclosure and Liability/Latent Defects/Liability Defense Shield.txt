For decades, owners of real estate were not obligated to disclose conditions about the property they were selling, relying on caveat emptor (let the buyer beware). Any mandatory disclosures were usually limited to disclosure of latent defects. Agents would say to their sellers, "Is there anything wrong with your property that I or a reasonably prudent buyer would not be able to recognize?"

Latent defects can include a leaking roof, damage from wood-destroying insects, water in the basement, foundation defects, age of the home, type of flooring, condition of the roof, nature of the drywall, presence of synthetic stucco, or the presence of mold.

Latent defect: Tree roots had penetrated the sewer pipe and the recommended solution was to replace the pipe from the house to the street; the sellers knew about this problem, but it would be difficult even for a plumber to discern.
Over the last few years, court decisions have moved away from caveat emptor to owner disclose and buyer discover. The South Carolina Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement places full responsibility on the owner of certain residential properties to disclose that of which the owner has knowledge of problems that need to be fixed/repaired.

"There oughta be a law" led many states, including South Carolina, to mandate disclosures by owners. As a practice to reduce risk to all parties in the transaction, the licensee should encourage the owner to make full and complete disclosure of any known problems with the property.

The burden to disclose is placed squarely on the party who actually knows about the problems—the owner! Licensees can view the Property Condition Disclosure Statement as a "liability defense shield."
