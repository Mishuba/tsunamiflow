Single Agency
 — Single agency results when the real estate brokerage firm represents only one client in the same transaction (the seller or the buyer). A listing agreement is used when the firm represents the seller, and a buyer representation agreement is used when the firm represents the buyer.

Dual Agency —
 Dual agency results when the real estate brokerage firm represents two clients (the seller and the buyer) in one transaction. Dual agency is illegal without disclosure and consent before a contract is formed between the parties. The only type of dual agency recognized by South Carolina law is disclosed dual agency. A general example is when the firm represents both the buyer and the seller in the same transaction. Examples include the following:
One licensee within the firm represents both the seller and the buyer
Two licensees within the firm represents both the buyer and the seller
Either way, the same firm represents both the buyer and the seller in the same transaction; thus, it is dual agency.

Disclosed Dual Agency
 — In disclosed dual agency, the firm's representation is limited due to a recognized conflict of interest. Each Dual Agency Agreement contains the names of both the seller and the buyer and identifies the property. The consent must be signed by the buyers before they write the offer and by the sellers before they sign the sales contract.
