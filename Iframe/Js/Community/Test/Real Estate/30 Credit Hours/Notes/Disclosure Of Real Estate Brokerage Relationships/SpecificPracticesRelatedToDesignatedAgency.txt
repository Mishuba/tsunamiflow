No firm has to offer designated agency. Whether or not the brokerage firm chooses to practice designated agency is always a company decision. South Carolina license law prescribes some specific requirements when brokerage firms opt to offer designated agency. They include all of the following practices and policies:
The firm must create policies and procedures reasonably calculated to ensure each client is represented in accordance with the requirements of this chapter. SC Code of Laws 40-57-350(J)(1).
 There may be no imputation of knowledge or information between and among the BIC, agents, and the clients. Designated agents may not disclose, except to the designated agent's BIC or appointed representative, information made confidential. SC Code of Laws 40-57-350(J)(9).
 A BIC cannot act as a designated agent in transactions where the BIC is either the representative of the seller or buyer and both clients are represented by the firm. The BIC must act as a dual agent. SC Code of Laws 40-57-350(J)(5).
 All other associated agents in the firm, as well as the BIC, remain as dual agents, even when designated agents have been appointed. SC Code of Laws 40-57-350(J)(8).
 When each branch office has a separate, different BIC, an affiliate licensee from one branch office may represent one party and an affiliate licensee from another branch office may represent the other party.

BIC Alert
BICs should consider if they want to provide the option for designated agency. If so, the BIC should discuss how to implement the BICs office policy.
