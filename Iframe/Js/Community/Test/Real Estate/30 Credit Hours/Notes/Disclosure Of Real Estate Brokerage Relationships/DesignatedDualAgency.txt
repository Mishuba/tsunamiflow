Designated dual agency provides clients with more representation, but it still requires disclosure and consent from both clients.
In designated agency, the BIC (or designated representative) may appoint individual associated licensees to act solely on behalf of each client. The BIC may create designated agency by the adoption of written office policies. SC Code of Laws §40-57-350(J). Here are several "rules" that must be followed for proper, legal designated dual agency:
The firm must create policies and procedures to protect the following of both clients: confidentiality, personal information, and motivational information of the clients.
An affiliated licensee may not be designated to represent one party if the licensee already has knowledge about the other party that is confidential, personal, and/or motivational. Any of this information may not be shared with anyone except the BIC.
A BIC cannot be involved in a transaction as a designated agent. The BIC remains a dual agent.
All other agents in the firm and the BIC remain as dual agents, even when designated agents have been appointed.
