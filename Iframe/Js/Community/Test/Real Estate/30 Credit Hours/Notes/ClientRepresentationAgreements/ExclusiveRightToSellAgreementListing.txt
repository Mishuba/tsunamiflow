The listing agreement establishes the relationship between the BIC (not the salesperson) and the property owner(s). It also addresses the issues of marketing, payment of fees, and the duties of the owner(s) and the BIC. Listing agreement forms may vary from office to office, so it is very important that licensees work with the BIC to use the correct form.

In the listing agreement, the BIC agrees to market the property and the sellers agree to pay the broker a fee when they enter into a purchase agreement. Although the fee is earned at that point, traditionally, the fee is paid at closing.

The listing agreement used https://static.kaplanlearn.com/assets/b8/51/220_ExclusiveRighttoSell_bw.pdf is in use in South Carolina and it includes provisions that will be common to other similar forms in the state. It is copyrighted by the South Carolina Association of REALTORS

Carefully study the agreement to understand the clauses and its implications to prospective sellers. Pay special attention to the compensation and terms paragraphs. If the sellers raise an issue that is unfamiliar to you and they want further interpretation, carefully explain that you are not an attorney, nor a party to the agreement, and refer to the BIC for further information.

Generally, a listing agreement is considered a unilateral contract. The BIC promises to market the property but does not promise to sell the property. The owners promise to pay the BIC if/when a buyer is found for the property, but the owners do not promise to pay anything if no buyer is found. As a reference, a purchase agreement, between the sellers and the buyers, is considered a bilateral contract: the sellers promise to sell and the buyers promise to buy.

PROVISIONS OF THE agreement
Read through each of the following sections and think about how to explain/discuss each paragraph with sellers who are ready to list with your firm/BIC. Anticipate questions from the sellers and frame your responses accordingly.
1. Consent to dual agency/designated agency: Will your BIC accept a listing if the sellers do not accept the possibility of dual agency? Think about how to respond if you are the listing agent, and these sellers go under contract and become buyers who want you to represent them. Also, what if they want to consider other properties listed with your BIC? Think about how to discuss confidentiality issues. 

2. Compensation to other brokerages. Help the seller recognize the value of working with buyers agents from other firms. You might say something like this: "In order to expose your property to the greatest number of potential buyers, my firm will place information about your home in our local Multiple Listing Service (MLS). It is entirely possible that another agency will be representing a buyer who is most interested in your home. My firm and I will cooperate with the other agent, sharing with the other agency the commissions you pay us. You will have the highest level of representation from us and it will not cost you anything more." 

3. Terms. This paragraph itemizes the listing/asking price and the broker fee. Although the commission is earned when the seller and buyer enter a purchase agreement, generally, the broker agrees to defer payment until closing. The BIC wants a protection period, during which time the seller agrees to pay a commission if a buyer who saw the property during the listing period enters into a purchase agreement after the listing expires. Generally, the protection period expires if the seller enters into a listing agreement with another BIC.

4. Earnest money. Although not required to form a valid contract, most buyers will offer earnest money to show that they are serious about their offer. Although a BIC can hold the money, most brokers prefer that the closing agent hold and disperse the money. Issues can arise when either the buyers or sellers decide to back out of the contract. The BIC will not authorize release of this money without agreement (signatures) from all parties. 

5. Signs. The sellers authorize placing the BICs For Sale signs on the property. Sometimes, sellers are hesitant, and the listing agent should be prepared to help these sellers recognize the marketing value of such signs.

6. Broker's duty. The agent should take some time to explain that the BIC is not guaranteeing that the home will sell. The broker will market the property and cooperate with other brokers to bring a sale, but the broker cannot guarantee that the property will be sold during this time frame. In this paragraph, the broker affirms that she will keep confidential information confidential, unless authorized by the seller or a court of law to release the information.

7. Broker liability limitation. The owners recognize services and benefits provided by the broker, and agrees to compensate the broker for attorney or other fees arising from this transaction if the broker is sued.

8. Owner's duty. The owners commit to the following duties during the time of the listing agreement:
<li>Provide reliable information about the ownership and operation of the property</li>
<li>Not talk to potential buyers or other brokers, but to refer all such conversations to the listing broker</li>
<li>Permit inspections and showing of the property</li>
<li>Adhere to fair housing laws (i.e., to show the property to potential buyers without regard to age, sex, race, creed, color, religion, national origin, handicap, or familial status)</li>
<li>Reimburse broker for reasonable expenditures for repairs and maintenance</li>
<li>Allow closing agent to pay the broker's commission from owner's proceeds at closing</li>
<li>Grant the broker permission to publish sale data pertaining to the sale and closing</li>
<li>Permit the broker to take photographs of the property for advertising and marketing purposes</li>
<li>Convey marketable title to the buyer in fee simple free from all liens, except those noted</li>
<li>Allow owner's attorney and settlement agent to furnish the broker with the final settlement statement</li>
<li>Not deal directly with any prospective buyers or cooperating brokers and refer all buyer inquiries to the listing broker</li>
<li>Authorize broker to reveal existence of offers on the property in response to buyers or cooperating brokers </li>
<li>Furnish the broker with written instructions regarding the confidentiality of information upon termination or completion of this agreement</li>

9. Property Information/Seller's Property Disclosure Statement. The owner is responsible for disclosing any material defects, whether hidden or obvious, and to furnish the BIC with a completed Owner's Property Disclosure Statement. The licensee taking the listing should discuss with the owner the implications of false information or trying to avoid making disclosures. The responsibility for disclosure rests entirely on the owner of the property, not the BIC or his agents.</p><p>
10. Disclosure. The owner authorizes the broker to disclose information to the broker's agents, prospective buyers, and inquiring parties. Such parties can include anyone having a lien against the property, such as a mortgage holder, and allows them to disclose information about such lien.</p><p>
11. <strong>Taxes</strong>. Owner agrees to comply with the tax provisions.</p><p>
12. <strong>Coastal Tidelands &amp; Wetlands Act</strong>. Owners of properties affected by this act agree to provide an addendum addressing those issues and to negotiate the cost of appropriate surveys with the buyers.</p><p>
13. <strong>MLS</strong>. Owner agrees to permit the property to be marketed in the local MLS and to allow compensation to any agent who brings a buyer via the MLS.</p><p>
14. <strong>Agreement for Lockbox</strong>. The listing agent should explain the advantages of instant access to the property via a lockbox. However, the owner is the one who determines if the broker can install a lockbox. At this time, the agent is advised to discuss putting away extra (and valuable) personal property.</p>
15. <strong>Internet marketing</strong>. The owner must agree to placing the property on the Internet and to share data with other MLS members for marketing and advertising purposes only.</p>
<p>16. <strong>Other offers</strong>. Owner agrees that the broker must present all offers to purchase to the owner until closing or expiration of the listing agreement.</p>
<p>17. <strong>Marketing the property</strong>. The broker will not market the property after the owner accepts an offer unless directed by the owner to continue marketing.</p>
<p>18. <strong>No control of commission rates or fees</strong>. All fees involved are a matter of negotiation between the broker and the owner, and are not fixed nor controlled by custom or MLS rules.</p>
<p>19. <strong>Maintenance</strong>. Owner agrees to maintain the property until the day of closing and that all the systems will be operative on the day of closing or possession, whichever comes first. Owner is responsible for delivering the premises to the buyer with no broken windows, torn or missing door or window screens, and no missing hardware, lighting or plumbing fixtures.</p>
<p>20. <strong>Agreement to sell</strong>. After entering into a purchase agreement, the owner is responsible for title examination, curing of any defects, proration of taxes, rents, and property expenses.</p>
<p>21. <strong>Lead-based paint</strong>. The owners of homes built before 1978 must provide the buyers with the lead-based paint disclosure form indicating any known presence of lead in the home. Owners must provide the buyers with the booklet <em>Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home</em>. </p>
<p>22. <strong>Mediation clause</strong>. Owner agrees to mediation if there are any issues relating to this agreement, for a period of up to 120 days after closing.</p>
<p>23. <strong>Fair housing</strong>. Owner recognizes that the broker will offer this property for sale without regard to race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin in full compliance with state and federal fair housing laws.</p>
<p>24. <strong>Facsimile</strong>. Parties agree to communicate offers and acceptances via the Internet, including signatures, initials, and handwritten or typewritten modifications.</p>
<p>25. <strong>Enforcement</strong>. Owner agrees to compensate the broker for any costs related to enforcing this agreement.</p>
<p>26. <strong>Sex offender/criminal information</strong>. Seller/owner agrees that the broker is not responsible for obtaining and disclosing information in the South Carolina Sex Offender Registry.</p>
<p>After all is said and done, the owner(s) and a representative for the real estate firm sign the listing agreement.</p>
