The exclusive right to buy/Buyer Agency Contract establishes the relationship between the BIC (not the salesperson) and potential buyers. While somewhat similar to the listing agreement, the buyer broker agreement addresses completely different concerns: locating properties, working exclusively with the broker, and various methods of compensation. The buyer agrees to conduct all negotiations through the BIC, including property shown or negotiated, or information requested by the buyer.

The BIC and her agents agree to locate the property, and the buyers agree to pay the broker's fee (or retainer fee or service fee). Although the fee is earned at the time buyers and sellers enter into a purchase agreement, traditionally, the fee is paid at closing. Buyer agency agreements vary from office to office, so it is very important that licensees work with their BIC to use the form preferred by their BIC.

The buyer agency agreement used https://static.kaplanlearn.com/assets/ad/54/130_ExclusiveRighttoBuy_bw.pdf is a form in use in South Carolina and it includes provisions that will be common to other similar forms in the state. It is copyrighted by the South Carolina Association of REALTORS

Buyer brokerage is still relatively new in the industry, so many buyers will be unfamiliar with making a firm commitment to work with only one agent. Licensees should carefully study the agreement so they can discuss each of the clauses and to explain the implications to their prospective buyers. They should pay special attention to the compensation and terms paragraphs. If the buyers raise an issue that is unfamiliar to the licensee, the licensee should carefully explain that the licensee is not an attorney or the party to the agreement and get back to the BIC for further information.

Generally, a buyer broker agreement is considered a unilateral  contract. The BIC promises to locate properties and to represent the buyer. The buyers promise to pay the BIC if/when they find a property, but the buyers do not promise to pay anything if they do not find a satisfactory property. As a reference, a purchase agreement, between the sellers and the buyers, is considered a bilateral contract: the sellers promise to sell and the buyers promise to buy.

PROVISIONS OF THE AGREEMENT
<p>Read through each of the following sections and think about how you will explain/discuss each paragraph with buyers who are ready to work exclusively with you and your firm/BIC. Anticipate questions from the buyers and frame your responses accordingly.</p>
<p>1. <strong>Appointment of broker</strong>. Buyers agree to conduct all negotiations through the BIC and will refer all inquiries from other agents to the BIC. In this contract, "negotiation" means property shown, negotiated, or information requested by the buyers through the broker.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Purpose of agency</strong>. Buyers generally describe the type of property that they seek to purchase or lease, including any personal property. They also indicate the price range, general location, and preferred terms, as well as any other specific concerns. Licensees should be aware that some information of what the buyers initially provide will change during the course of the relationship. Thus, the licensee may say, "Promise me that I will be the first to know if you change any criteria that we have just discussed." (Hopefully, buyers will turn to their agent, not another licensee for additional information.)</p>
<p>3. <strong>Broker's duties</strong>. This paragraph now introduces the idea of dual agency. </p>
<li>The brokerage/agent promises to diligently and effectively locate property that is within the buyers' price range and located in the general area indicated by the buyers. </li>
<li>If the broker is not also representing the seller, then the broker will solely represent the buyer.</li>
<li>If the broker is also representing the seller (dual agency), the broker will not disclose to the buyer any information gained from the seller that is confidential and within the trust of the fiduciary relationship with the seller, nor will the broker disclose such information gained in trust from the buyer.</li>
<li>The broker may also (and probably) will be representing buyers who are looking for the same type of property. The agent should emphasize keeping confidential information confidential, not discussed to other parties.</li>
<p>4. <strong>Buyer's duties</strong>. Licensees should spend some time discussing the commitment introduced in this paragraph. Many buyers are unaware of how real estate agents get paid, so they do not understand the importance of loyalty and viewing properties only with their agent. In some real estate communities, agents from other brokerages will honor a verbal, "I'm working with so-and-so," but many do not.</p>
<li>Agents can avoid complications by accompanying buyer clients when they enter open houses, and gain their commitment that they will call their agent and not the listing agent when they see an advertisement or a sign on a property in which they might be interested.</li>
<li>Buyer clients must provide the broker/licensees with reliable information, including financial information, necessary for the broker to locate desirable properties. </li>
<li>Buyer clients must provide the broker with the general nature, location, and any other special requirements of the desired property as well as the price range.</li>
<li>Buyer clients must provide permission for the broker and settlement agent to receive copies of the Closing Disclosure (previously final HUD-1 settlement statement).</li>
<p>5. <strong>Compensation of broker</strong>. While the compensation is deemed earned at the time buyers and sellers enter into a purchase agreement, generally, payment is delayed until closing or buyer default. Buyers can choose from some of the following choices as to how their agent is paid: </p>
<li>Retainer fee. Buyers pay a nonrefundable fee up-front, similar to that which attorneys charge, which may or may not be credited against the brokerage fee.</li>
<li>Service fee. Buyers agree to a specific fee which may or may not be derived from the cooperative fee offered by the listing agent.</li>
<li>Brokerage fee. A certain amount or percentage when the buyer buys or leases property negotiated by the broker, of listed or for sale by owner properties. This paragraph also includes a "protection period" during which the fee is still owed if the broker showed the property during the original time frame.</li>
<li>Brokerage fee. The buyer broker is paid by the brokerage fee from the transaction. In this situation, the most common, the buyer is not responsible for paying the broker the brokerage fee.</li>
<p>Buyer agents should really spend time discussing these options with their buyers and to stress the importance of remaining loyal to their buyer agent by not entering an open house without their agent and to call their agent, and not the brokerage listed on For Sale signs.</p>
<p>6. <strong>Term of agency.</strong> States the beginning and ending of the commitment between the buyer and the real estate agent.</p>
<p>7. <strong>Consent to dual agency/designated agency</strong>. Buyer acknowledges receiving the South Carolina Disclosure of Real Estate Brokerage Relationships form. Buyer then acknowledges that the agent may approach the buyer with authorization for dual agency or designated agency. </p>
<p>The buyers can respond to the following options:</p>
<li>Will not consider dual agency</li>
<li>May consider dual agency when provided with additional information about the other party/sellers, in which case they will need to sign a separate written Designated Agency Agreement</li>
<li>Will not consider designated agency</li>
<li>May consider designated agency when provided additional information about the other party/sellers, in which case they will need to sign a separate written Designated Agency Agreement</li>
<p>Agents should consider if their BIC will accept a buyer if the buyers do not accept the possibility of dual or designated agency. Think about how to respond if you are the buyer agent, and the buyers want to see a property that you have just listed. Think about how to discuss confidentiality issues.</p>
<p>8. <strong>Other potential buyers</strong>. Explain to buyers that they are likely to have competition from buyers looking for similar properties; in fact, you may be working with such buyers. Explain what and how information will be kept confidential.</p>
<p>9. <strong>Indemnification of broker</strong>. Buyers promise to not enter into other brokerage agreements and to disclose any properties they may have viewed prior to entering into this agreement.</p>
<p>10. <strong>Disclosure of buyer's identity/confidentiality</strong>. This is a very important paragraph to discuss with buyers who need to decide if the agent can tell the listing agent/sellers the buyers' names. Additionally, buyers need to be aware that once their offer is not confidential, the sellers may authorize their agent to disclose information about the offer to other parties.</p>
<p>11. <strong>Broker liability limitation</strong>. Buyers agree to hold the broker harmless and to assist with legal fees if necessary, unless the claim that is brought is for an intentional or willful act. The owners recognize services and benefits provided by the broker and agrees to compensate the broker for attorney or other fees arising from this transaction if the broker is sued.</p>
<p>12. <strong>Nondiscrimination</strong>. Buyers recognize that this transaction will be in full compliance with local, state, and federal fair housing laws against discrimination on the basis of race, creed, color, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, marital status, age, or disabilities.</p>
<p>13. <strong>Professional counsel</strong>. Buyers recognize that the broker is only a real estate agent and not an attorney, tax advisor, lender, appraiser, surveyor, structural engineer, home inspector, or other professional service provider. Licensees should be prepared to advise the buyer to seek professional advice concerning the condition of the property, or legal, tax, and other professional service matters.</p>
<p>14. <strong>Mediation clause</strong>. Buyers agree to mediation if there are any issues relating to this agreement, for a period of up to 120 days after closing.</p>
<p>15. <strong>Sex offender/criminal information</strong>. Buyers agree that they and not the broker are responsible for obtaining information in the South Carolina Sex Offender Registry. Buyers are solely responsible for researching any information about the following: sex offender, death, psychological stigma, clandestine laboratory, and crime information.</p>
<p>16.<strong>Entire binding agreement</strong>. This is the total agreement and can only be changed by a subsequent written document, signed by both parties.</p>
<p>17. <strong>Contingencies</strong>. (If any.)</p>
<p>18. <strong>Facsimile</strong>. Parties agree to communicate offers and acceptances via the Internet, including signatures, initials, and handwritten or typewritten modifications.</p>
<p>After all is said and done, the buyers sign the buyer agreement as does a representative for the real estate firm.</p>
